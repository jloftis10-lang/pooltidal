A green pool looks like a disaster, but it's rarely a permanent one. What it is, though, is a process — not a single treatment that clears the water overnight. If you've just discovered your pool has gone green (after a vacation, a stretch of neglect, or inheriting a pool that hasn't been touched in a while), here's what actually happens during recovery and roughly how long it takes.

## What's really going on in a green pool

"Green" almost always means algae has taken hold, usually because chlorine levels dropped low enough (or stayed low long enough) for it to establish. Algae multiplies fast in warm water, which is exactly the conditions San Diego provides most of the year. Once it's visibly green, you're not dealing with a small chemical imbalance anymore — you're dealing with an active algae bloom that has to be killed off, filtered out, and then kept from coming back.

## The general timeline

For a pool that's green but not black or swampy, a fairly typical recovery looks like:

1. **Day 1 — Shock and initial treatment.** The water gets shocked with a strong dose of chlorine to kill the algae. It usually gets cloudier or murkier before it gets better, as dead algae clouds the water.
2. **Day 1–2 — Continuous filtration.** The filter needs to run around the clock (not on a normal daily timer) to physically remove the dead algae and debris the shock treatment knocked loose. This is often the step that gets skipped in DIY attempts, and it's the one that actually clears the water.
3. **Day 2–3 — Brushing and vacuuming.** Algae that's settled on walls and the floor gets brushed loose and vacuumed out, since dead algae sitting on surfaces can reintroduce nutrients that feed a new bloom.
4. **Ongoing — Retesting and rebalancing.** Chemistry gets retested regularly through the process, since a heavy shock treatment swings pH and other levels around, and those need to be brought back in range as the water clears.

For a moderately green pool, 2–4 days of this process is common. Severely green or swampy pools — especially ones that sat neglected for weeks or months — can take longer and sometimes need a second round of shock and filtration.

## What determines how long it actually takes

- **How long the pool has been neglected** — a pool that turned green last week clears faster than one that's been green for two months
- **Filter type and condition** — a clean, properly sized filter clears water much faster than one that's old, undersized, or already struggling
- **Pool size relative to equipment** — larger pools take longer to fully turn over and filter
- **Whether equipment is actually working** — recovery assumes the pump and filter can run continuously; if either was part of why the pool went green in the first place (a failing pump, a clogged filter that was never addressed), that has to be fixed before recovery can finish

## Why DIY green pool recovery often stalls out

The chemistry part (shocking the water) is the easy part to get right. What usually goes wrong is the filtration step — running the filter continuously for 24–48+ hours puts real strain on equipment that may not have been maintained well to begin with, and it's easy to declare victory too early once the water looks clear but is still cloudy with suspended algae that will bloom right back.
