Ask five pool owners what they pay for weekly service and you'll get five different numbers — often for reasons that have nothing to do with who's overcharging or underpricing. Pool cleaning cost isn't a single fixed rate; it moves based on a handful of specific factors about the pool itself. Here's what actually drives it.

## Pool size

More water and more surface area means more time per visit — more area to skim and brush, more chemical volume to balance, more time vacuuming if it's needed. A small plunge pool and a large lap pool are not the same job, even if both get a weekly visit.

## Pool type and equipment

A standard chlorine pool and a saltwater pool both get tested and balanced, but a saltwater system's salt cell needs its own periodic check as part of a thorough visit. Pools with an attached spa add a second body of water with its own chemistry and, often, its own jets and heater — that's additional time on every visit, not a one-time thing.

## Current condition

This is the one that surprises people most. A pool that's been maintained consistently is a straightforward weekly visit. A pool that's cloudy, has algae starting to form, or hasn't been serviced in a while usually needs an initial recovery period — extra chemical treatment, more filtration cycles, sometimes more than one visit — before it settles into a normal routine and a normal rate. Starting condition affects the first few visits more than the ongoing weekly price.

## Access

A pool that's easy to reach — clear gate access, no obstacles, nothing blocking the equipment pad — is quicker to service than one that requires working around locked gates, side-yard obstacles, or hard-to-reach equipment. This is a smaller factor than size or condition, but it's a real one.

## What's actually included matters as much as the number

A quote that looks lower on paper isn't necessarily the better deal if it covers less. Our [weekly service](/services/weekly-cleaning-maintenance) includes water chemistry testing and balancing, skimming, wall and tile brushing, vacuuming as needed, basket cleaning, and an equipment check on every visit — not an upsell list of add-ons billed separately. When comparing quotes, ask specifically what's covered in the base visit versus what costs extra.
