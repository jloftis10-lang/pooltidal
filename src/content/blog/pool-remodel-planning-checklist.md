Good pool-remodel planning is less about arriving with every finish selected and more about documenting the current condition, priorities, and site constraints. Use this checklist to make the first estimate conversation specific.

## Document the pool as it is now

- Take wide photos from several angles
- Photograph the surface, waterline tile, coping, deck transitions, steps, benches, skimmers, lights, and visible damage
- Note approximate pool and spa dimensions
- List known leaks, cracks, rough areas, stains, loose materials, or drainage concerns
- Record anything that has changed recently

If a leak or equipment failure is the main issue, begin with the [repair-versus-remodel guide](/blog/pool-repair-vs-remodel).

## Separate needs from preferences

Create two lists:

1. Conditions that must be corrected
2. Appearance or convenience upgrades you would like to consider

This helps proposals show what is essential and what could be phased or removed. Consider the interior finish, tile, coping, deck, lighting, features, automation, heating, sanitizing system, and accessibility.

## Record site and access conditions

Note gate widths, stairs, slopes, driveway access, tight side yards, overhead restrictions, landscaping, neighboring-property considerations, pets, and likely staging areas. Do not assume access is a minor detail; share it early.

## Questions for the written proposal

Ask what preparation, materials, demolition, disposal, plumbing, startup, and cleanup are included. Confirm how allowances work, how changes are approved, what the schedule assumes, and who handles permits or inspections when applicable. Request clarification for vague line items before comparing totals.
