The pump is running — you can hear it, maybe even feel it humming — but nothing's coming out of the returns, or the pressure gauge reads low, or the water just isn't moving the way it should. This is one of the more common pool equipment complaints, and it usually traces back to one of a few specific causes.

## Air is getting into the system

If the pump basket or pump lid isn't sealing properly, or there's a loose fitting on the suction side, the pump can pull air instead of water — you'll often see air bubbles in the pump basket or hear a different pitch from the motor. This is one of the more common causes of "running but not moving water" and is often a fix rather than a replacement: a cracked lid O-ring, a loose union, or a suction-side leak letting air in before the water reaches the pump.

## The skimmer or pump basket is clogged

Leaves, debris, and buildup in the skimmer basket or pump strainer basket restrict how much water can reach the pump in the first place. If the pump is starved of water, it can run without moving much of anything. This is the easiest thing to check yourself — pull both baskets and clear them out before assuming it's something more serious.

## The filter is clogged or the valve is in the wrong position

A heavily clogged filter restricts flow on the return side, which can make it seem like the pump isn't working even though it's the filter holding things back. Check the filter pressure gauge — if it's reading high compared to normal, that's usually the filter needing a cleaning or backwash. Also worth a quick check: multiport valves and actuators can occasionally end up in the wrong position, restricting or redirecting flow without it being obvious from the pump itself.

## The impeller is clogged or damaged

Debris — a small rock, a piece of plastic, buildup — can lodge in the pump's impeller and either block it partially or damage the blades. This one usually needs the pump opened up to diagnose properly, which is where it's worth having someone take a look rather than guessing.

## The pump itself may be failing

If none of the above explain it, the pump motor or seals could be the actual problem — a failing motor can spin without generating enough force to move water efficiently, especially as it ages. This is the scenario where diagnosis matters most: replacing a pump that didn't need replacing is a real cost, and so is missing a genuinely failing pump and letting it run inefficiently (or fail completely) in the meantime.
